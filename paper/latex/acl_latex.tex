\begin{abstract}
Accuracy changes after removing or swapping an image do not by themselves show that a multimodal LLM uses the image correctly; they may reflect response sensitivity alone. We test image dependence by comparing ablation against the answer prior, scoring donor-image swaps against the donor key, and requiring an image-only positive control on brain-MRI questions from the LUMIERE glioblastoma cohort.

A reference model reads sequence and lesion side from the image (\vfourEoneOwnAIAGemini{}\% and \vfourEoneOwnLILGemini{}\%, versus \vfourEoneTextAIAGemini{}\% and \vfourEoneTextLILGemini{}\% without it) and tracks donor swaps, showing the tasks are feasible. Four open-weight models show limited image dependence: only one image-effect test survives multiplicity correction, its accuracy remains below the most common key's share, we did not detect donor-specific tracking under the original assignment, and none exceeds the best category lookup on the management rule. Two bf16 reruns remain near chance on the control, making 4-bit serving an unlikely sole explanation.

A motivating audit finds that a slice-based enhancement rule matches the expert RANO rating for \vfourConcordantDetPct{}\% of \vfourDeterminate{} determinate follow-ups, so those ratings are not treated as validated targets. We measure observable image-dependent behavior, not internal image use; keys are segmentation- or rule-derived and not clinician-validated.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Medical multimodal LLMs are often evaluated by final-answer accuracy, which cannot distinguish image-based reasoning from reliance on textual cues, option patterns, or guessing. Such shortcuts are well documented: models exploit annotation artifacts in natural language inference \citep{gururangan2018annotation}, syntactic heuristics \citep{mccoy2019right}, and unfaithful stated reasoning \citep{turpin2023language}. In chest-radiograph question answering, models can rely on linguistic patterns or irrelevant image regions \citep{nguyen2025localizing}. Image ablation or swapping is informative only when the displayed image contains enough evidence to recover the answer.

We ask when an image intervention measures image dependence rather than merely response sensitivity. An accuracy difference between an image arm and a text-only arm is confounded by the answer prior, and an answer that changes when the image is swapped need not move toward the answer the new image supports. We therefore read the ablation together with three additions: reference values for each phase; a matched donor swap scored against the donor's key with the no-image prior subtracted; and an audit that compares the key against an independent expert rating, which checks agreement between two labeling procedures rather than confirming that the key is recoverable from the displayed slices. Protocols that perturb the text or the image and report the change in accuracy, such as HEAL-MedVQA \citep{nguyen2025localizing}, address the same concern; we did not run their protocol, so the comparison is by design, not measured, and Section~\ref{sec:results} gives a worked case in which the ablation alone points to image use and the additions do not. We instantiate the questions on the five OmniBrainBench phases \citep{peng2026omnibrainbench} as same-patient chains from the LUMIERE glioblastoma cohort \citep{suter2022lumiere}: anatomical and imaging assessment (AIA), lesion identification and localization (LIL), diagnostic synthesis and causal reasoning (DSCR), prognostic judgment and risk forecasting (PJRF), and therapeutic cycle management (TCM); Figure~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the design. The chain is an extension that lets us also test whether a model uses stated facts and upstream context in the management step. A motivating audit of the expert RANO ratings found that they cannot be treated as validated targets for the displayed slices (Section~\ref{sec:audit}), which motivates an evaluation with explicit key provenance and fixed controls (Sections~\ref{sec:v4}--\ref{sec:controls}).

The paper makes three contributions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt, topsep=2pt]
    \item A protocol that keeps three outcomes apart, accuracy benefit, output sensitivity and donor tracking, and reads image ablation against the answer prior, with a worked case (Section~\ref{sec:results}) in which the ablation alone credits Llama-4-Scout with image use on the diagnosis category although its accuracy with the image stays below the most common key's share and its donor gain is not separable from a random donor assignment.
    \item An item set with an explicit origin for each key, built on LUMIERE, and a motivating audit of the expert labels: an enhancement-based rule reproduces the expert RANO category for \vfourConcordantDetPct{}\% of the \vfourDeterminate{} follow-ups on which it is determinate (coverage is reported separately: it is undetermined for \vfourUndetermined{} of \vfourFollowups{}), so we do not treat those ratings as validated targets for the displayed slices; the audit does not show which side is wrong. The released OmniBrainBench groupings do not give same-patient chains (only 2 verifiable cross-phase links among 6,823 questions).
    \item An evaluation of four open-weight models and one reference model under a prespecified control suite. The reference model shows image-dependent performance on sequence and lesion-side questions, whereas the open models are limited and inconsistent; on the diagnosis-category task the most common key covers \vfourMajorityDSCR{}\% of items, so accuracy there is hard to separate from a class prior even for the reference model. As an extension, the chain tests the use of stated facts and upstream context on the management step.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Shortcuts and faithfulness.} Models can reach correct labels without performing the intended reasoning. Hypothesis-only baselines exploit annotation artifacts in NLI datasets \citep{poliak2018hypothesis,gururangan2018annotation}, models adopt heuristics that fail on HANS \citep{mccoy2019right}, and biased input features can alter chain-of-thought answers without appearing in the stated rationale \citep{turpin2023language}.

\paragraph{Answering without the image.} Visual question-answering models often exploit language priors. \citet{goyal2017making} countered this by pairing questions with images yielding different answers, while \citet{chen2024right} show that many multimodal benchmark items do not require visual content. Similar failures appear in medicine: \citet{yan2025worse} report severe degradation under adversarial diagnostic counterparts, \citet{jin2024hidden} find flawed rationales in 35.5\% of GPT-4V's correct answers, and \citet{nguyen2025localizing} (HEAL-MedVQA) use textual and visual perturbations to localize shortcuts in chest-radiograph QA. We extend this logic to brain-MRI questions and add a donor swap scored against the donor's key with the no-image prior subtracted, per-phase reference values, and a key-recoverability audit. Unlike correctness on complementary pairs \citep{goyal2017making}, which requires the model to answer each image correctly, this statistic measures movement toward what the swapped image supports and so is informative even for models that are rarely correct, as ours are; we did not run HEAL-MedVQA's protocol, so that comparison is one of design. The combination adds discrimination a raw gain lacks: a non-specific image shift and a noisy tracker both reach \vfourDonorValShiftGain{} points of gain and change a comparable share of answers, yet the conditional donor-label permutation flags them at \vfourDonorValShiftDetect{}\% and \vfourDonorValTrackLowDetect{}\% (Table~\ref{tab:donorval}). The same-patient chain extends the questions to interventions on supplied clinical facts. UniMod \citep{gu2026unimod} addresses modality imbalance during training by supervising image-only, text-only, and fused predictions separately; we instead evaluate existing models for observable image dependence.

\paragraph{Brain benchmarks and longitudinal data.} OmniBrainBench \citep{peng2026omnibrainbench} contains 6,823 closed-ended brain-imaging questions spanning five clinical phases and 15 modalities. Its supplement describes a patient-level ADNI extension, but we did not evaluate it. LUMIERE \citep{suter2022lumiere} and MU-Glioma-Post \citep{muglioma2025post} provide longitudinal glioma imaging, treatment, and outcome data but no question layer.





\section{Building Chains from LUMIERE}
\label{sec:lumiere}

\paragraph{Why not OmniBrainBench.} A chain needs one patient's phases. We checked whether any image is shared across questions of different phases within a grouped case: of 6,823 closed-ended questions only 2 verifiable cross-phase patient links exist, and a second method based on filename identifiers agreed; the counts are lower bounds (Appendix~\ref{sec:app-compat}). Each sub-corpus was built for one task, so a patient is essentially asked one phase-type of question. This is an incompatibility between the released groupings and our evaluation, not a defect in a promise its authors made.

\paragraph{LUMIERE.} LUMIERE \citep{suter2022lumiere} has 91 glioblastoma patients with longitudinal MRI (T1, contrast-enhanced T1, T2, FLAIR), automated segmentations (DeepBraTumIA and HD-GLIO-AUTO; \citealp{suter2022lumiere, kickingereder2019automated}), RANO ratings by one neuroradiologist \citep{wen2010updated}, and overall survival for most patients. Segmentations are automated and were not manually verified; the ratings are expert-assigned.

\subsection{Motivating audit: can an automated rule reproduce the expert ratings from the displayed slices?}
\label{sec:audit}

The natural key for a diagnosis-category question is the expert RANO rating, but whether that rating can be recovered from the displayed slices is untested. We tested it with an automated reproducibility audit: for each first-line follow-up we tracked one enhancing lesion across scans, measured it on its own largest axial cross-section, computed the RANO bidimensional product and applied the enhancement criteria (progression: measurable disease at least 25\% above the smallest earlier measurement, the nadir, or measurable disease where the nadir had none; response against the post-operative baseline; measurable means both diameters at least 10 mm). The rule has no new-lesion criterion beyond that. Coverage: of \vfourFollowups{} first-line follow-ups, \vfourUndetermined{} are undetermined because no measurable enhancing disease exists on the baseline or nadir scan, so the rule abstains on them. Agreement, our primary number, is computed over the \vfourDeterminate{} determinate follow-ups: the rule reproduces the expert category for \vfourConcordant{} (\vfourConcordantDetPct{}\%, Wilson 95\% interval \vfourConcordantDetLo{} to \vfourConcordantDetHi{}\%, and \vfourAuditClusterLo{} to \vfourAuditClusterHi{}\% when the \vfourAuditPatients{} patients are resampled instead; counting the undetermined ones as failures would give \vfourConcordantPct{}\%, which mixes abstention with disagreement). Most of the \vfourDisagree{} disagreements are the rule returning complete response where the expert rated progressive or stable disease. Disagreement has several possible sources that this audit does not separate; LUMIERE records that one neuroradiologist rated the studies but not what they used. Our automated product correlates only moderately with the rater's recorded target-lesion diameters (Spearman \vfourBpRho{} over \vfourBpN{} follow-ups), so measurement error is one plausible contributor, though the checkable subset was too small to establish a size-closeness difference either way (Appendix~\ref{sec:app-summary}). The audit therefore shows these ratings cannot be treated as validated targets for the displayed inputs; it does not measure how many are unanswerable, which would need qualified reviewers on a sample of the disagreements.

\subsection{The item set}
\label{sec:v4}

The keys have four different origins (Table~\ref{tab:phases}), and we make no uniform claim that each is a function of what the model sees: AIA is the sequence rendered; LIL and DSCR are derived from automated segmentation masks the model does not see (recoverable from the slices only if the segmentation is right; these are segmentation-derived targets reviewers have not validated); PJRF is the recorded outcome, scored probabilistically since a future outcome is not a function of the stated facts; and TCM is the output of a stated management rule, testing adherence rather than clinical correctness. All random choices use fixed seeds and never look at model output.

\paragraph{Cohort.} We select one first-line follow-up per patient (before any second surgery), in strata defined by the TCM rule class and the DSCR category, with quotas and seed fixed in advance, giving \vfourPatients{} patients (counts by phase and key in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-summary}). Selection used tabular records and segmentation masks only, and progressive-disease follow-ups within 2 weeks of the 12-week window are left out.

\paragraph{Images.} Each image is an atlas-space axial slice, skull-stripped by the dataset authors with HD-BET \citep{isensee2019automated}, at 4$\times$ magnification with a 20\,mm scale bar, in radiological orientation. Slice levels are those on which the key is computed.

\paragraph{AIA, LIL, DSCR.} AIA is the positive control: its key is the sequence rendered, balanced over four sequences (the most common covers \vfourMajorityAIA{}\% of items), so text alone can reach only chance in expectation. LIL's key is the hemisphere and anterior/posterior half of the largest tumor-core component on the displayed slice; lesions within 10\,mm of a midline, smaller than 100\,mm$^2$ or with under 80\% of their pixels on their centroid's side of the midline are not asked about. DSCR's stem states the RANO enhancement criteria, and its key is the category computed from the bidimensional products.

\paragraph{PJRF.} The prediction time is the follow-up scan and the outcome is death within 52 weeks of it, from the LUMIERE survival record. The model picks one of four probability bins (midpoints .10, .30, .50, .80) and is scored with the Brier score, not by whether it picked a ``correct'' bin. The base rate (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}) reflects our selection, not a population rate. The origin and censoring of the survival time are undocumented (Appendix~\ref{sec:app-handling}), so PJRF is secondary and no other claim in this paper rests on it.

\paragraph{TCM.} LUMIERE records no treatment after chemoradiotherapy, so a documented treatment is not available and the key is a rule-based recommendation, not what was done: non-progressive disease continues adjuvant temozolomide; progressive disease within 12 weeks of completing radiotherapy is confirmed with repeat MRI before any change; later progressive disease prompts a change of therapy. The base stems supply the clinical facts but not this rule; only a post hoc condition states the rule in the prompt (Section~\ref{sec:results}). The 12-week window is from the RANO criteria \citep{wen2010updated}; because we cannot assess the radiation field, the rule is a simplification (see Limitations). Radiotherapy dates are not in LUMIERE, so the stem states the weeks since chemoradiotherapy under an assumed standard schedule (ending 10 weeks after surgery). The same diagnosis therefore maps to different actions depending on the stated timing (\vfourTcmConfirm{} repeat-MRI and \vfourTcmEscalate{} change-therapy items among \vfourTcmPdItems{} progressive-disease items), which lets a lookup of the category alone be told apart from use of the timing fact.




\section{Controls and Pre-specified Analysis}
\label{sec:controls}

The target is \emph{correct, task-relevant image dependence under specified interventions}: whether a model's answer moves toward what the displayed image supports, not merely whether its output reacts to the image. Majority-baseline performance is contextual evidence for an accuracy gain, not a necessary test for image use: a model can read some items from the slice yet still score below the most common key by missing others, so accuracy below that share neither establishes nor refutes image use on its own.

The analysis plan was written before any model run; its hash is recorded in the project log, and the file is not externally timestamped. The plan was amended before the reported runs to correct two key-construction measurement errors (reducing the cohort to 46 patients) and the DSCR stem wording; the hypotheses, margin, correction family, seeds and decision rules were unchanged. Models are the four open-weight models (MedGemma-4B \citep{sellergren2025medgemma}, Gemma-3-12B and Gemma-3-27B \citep{gemmateam2025gemma3}, Llama-4-Scout; 4-bit, greedy decoding, 800 tokens; the reference model uses 16{,}384 tokens because hidden reasoning counts against the cap; unparseable responses score incorrect). Each condition asks one question with a manufactured upstream context, so a phase's effect is not mixed with the model's own earlier answers.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=1pt, topsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{E1, image effect} (AIA, LIL, DSCR): accuracy with the image minus text only, paired by patient, with a patient-bootstrap interval. The margin is $\pm10$ percentage points, fixed in advance; ``within margin'' requires the whole interval inside it, which at these sample sizes is reachable only when few items differ between the arms. Holm correction over the 12 model-phase tests; other contrasts are descriptive and unadjusted, with exact McNemar tests \citep{mcnemar1947note} and Wilson intervals \citep{wilson1927probable} where a proportion is reported. Because the bootstrap collapses to zero width when every pair is concordant, we also report, post hoc, an add-half interval for the paired difference \citep{agresti2005simple} and the exact $p$. \textbf{AIA is the positive control:} the plan's operational reading rule is that for a model whose unadjusted AIA interval is not above zero, the test has not been shown to detect image use, and E1 on LIL and DSCR supports no conclusion about it.
    \item \textbf{E2, donor tracking}: swap the image for a donor's with a different key (one donor per patient and phase, same number of images); $G=1[\text{swap}=\text{donor key}]-1[\text{text only}=\text{donor key}]$, so the no-image prior is subtracted. The estimand is conditional on the fixed assignment, in which a patient donates to at most two recipients. Because a positive gain can come from a generic response to any image rather than from the shown donor, we add a post hoc \emph{conditional label-association} permutation: holding each recipient's observed answers fixed, it treats the assigned donor-key labels as exchangeable among recipients subject to the swap constraint that no recipient receives its own key, and asks whether answers match the assigned donor's key more often than a key drawn from a different donor. It does not reconstruct the image-assignment mechanism, so it licenses only that narrow comparison. On synthetic predictors with known behaviour, the permutation flags a genuine tracker but not a generic image-presence shift and holds its false-positive rate at the nominal 5\% under a constraint-valid no-tracking null (Table~\ref{tab:donorval}; full checks, including a collision-repair validation, in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-summary}).
    \item \textbf{E3, use of stated facts} (TCM): with the true context, move the stated timing across the 12-week window (\emph{flip\_window}) or replace the stated category by one that maps to another action class (\emph{flip\_label}). The registered summary was the share of answers equal to the rule's new answer; because that counts answers that had not changed, we instead report each item's transition relative to the true-context answer (Table~\ref{tab:e3trans}) and take moved over informative items as the rate of interest. A category lookup alone reaches at most \vfourTcmLookupEsc{}\% accuracy (\vfourTcmLookupConf{}\% if it always confirms first) and cannot follow the timing flip.
    \item \textbf{E4, upstream context}: true, randomly wrong (a seeded incorrect option per earlier answer), or absent context.
    \item \textbf{E5, PJRF} (secondary; results in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-handling}): Brier score and skill against the leave-one-out base rate, and AUC.
\end{itemize}

We report three observable outcomes separately: accuracy benefit (E1), output sensitivity (the share of items whose answer letter differs between the image and text-only arms, and between the swapped and own image), and correct donor tracking (E2); none observes internal processing. Output sensitivity, the transition tables and the output-handling counts are post hoc, descriptive analyses. The ordinary five-phase chain (own answers as context) is run with and without images as a secondary analysis; each arm regenerates its upstream answers, so later-phase differences are total effects through the chain.

\paragraph{Reference and precision controls.} Three of the four open models did not meet the operational AIA gate (Section~\ref{sec:results}), so we added three post hoc controls, each reported apart from the four-model Holm family and run under the same prompts, temperature 0 and decision rules (tags and digests in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-handling}). (i)~A reference (positive-control) model, Gemini-3.6-Flash, on E1 and E2; empty API responses are counted and rerun (\vfourCtrlEmpty{} of \vfourCtrlRecords{} records), and, being an API model, it can change without notice. (ii)~A precision control, Gemma-3-27B rerun in bf16; its reading rule, fixed in advance, attributed part of the gap to quantization only if the bf16 AIA interval rose clearly above the 4-bit model's, but the serving stack also differs from the Ollama runs, so precision and stack are confounded (we draw no conclusion). (iii)~A non-Gemma control, Pixtral-12B (bf16), on E1 and E2 for AIA and LIL; its DSCR image conditions are excluded post hoc because the three-image prompt returned empty output for many records (Appendix~\ref{sec:app-handling}).

\paragraph{The reference model reads the image.} Gemini-3.6-Flash identifies the sequence for \vfourEoneOwnAIAGemini{}\% of patients with the image and \vfourEoneTextAIAGemini{}\% without (difference \vfourEoneDiffAIAGemini{} points, 95\% CI [\vfourEoneLoAIAGemini{}, \vfourEoneHiAIAGemini{}]), places the lesion for \vfourEoneOwnLILGemini{}\% against \vfourEoneTextLILGemini{}\%, and with the image swapped gives the donor's answer for \vfourEtwoSwapAIAGemini{}\% (AIA) and \vfourEtwoSwapLILGemini{}\% (LIL) of patients (Table~\ref{tab:ctrl}). A model that reads the slices can therefore answer these items, so the tasks are feasible; this does not show that every item is valid, nor why a weaker model fails. DSCR is different: the reference model scores \vfourEoneOwnDSCRGemini{}\% with the image and \vfourEoneTextDSCRGemini{}\% without, where the most common key covers \vfourMajorityDSCR{}\% of items, so most of its text-only accuracy is the class prior and it recovers the key for only \vfourEoneOwnDSCRGemini{}\% of items. We therefore cannot establish that DSCR is answerable from the slices for every item (the shortfall may be perception, keys the model does not see, or key error), so a null DSCR effect for another model cannot be read as a failure to use the image.

\paragraph{Open models: image effect (E1).} Sequence identification stays between \vfourEoneOwnAIAGemmaTwelve{} and \vfourEoneOwnAIAScout{}\% (chance 25\%); Table~\ref{tab:e1} gives every cell and Figure~\ref{fig:e1effect} the per-model effect with its interval. The operational AIA gate (an unadjusted interval above zero) is met only by Llama-4-Scout, and only on the bootstrap interval (exact McNemar $p=\vfourEonePAIAScout{}$). No conclusion depends on this borderline pass: the gate is a conservative screen, not a prerequisite for lesion or category reading, so the DSCR case below is read through the additions, and limited open-model image dependence holds whether or not Scout passes. One image-effect test survives Holm correction, Llama-4-Scout on DSCR, but it does not show the model reads the category from the slices: its accuracy with the image stays below the most common key's share (\vfourMajorityDSCR{}\%) and its text-only accuracy far below, so the difference gives the gap, not its mechanism. This below-majority accuracy is not a constant answer: class-wise (Table~\ref{tab:confusion}), Scout answers \vfourConfOnMajority{} of \vfourConfOnMajorityN{} progressive-disease items and recovers \vfourConfOffMajority{} of \vfourConfOffMajorityN{} non-majority items (complete-response recall \vfourConfRecallCR{}\%, stable-disease \vfourConfRecallSD{}\%), so scoring below the majority share reflects redistributed errors, not non-use of the image. The DSCR intervals above zero for MedGemma-4B and Gemma-3-12B do not survive correction; on LIL every interval includes zero. A test that fails correction is not evidence of no effect: no open-model AIA or LIL interval lies inside the $\pm10$-point margin. Each open model's AIA answers concentrate on one sequence (modal answer \vfourAiaModalShareLo{} to \vfourAiaModalShareHi{}\% of the \vfourNAIA{} items), consistent with a fixed default under weak image use, though every image reaches the model (Appendix~\ref{sec:app-handling}).

\paragraph{Precision control.} Rerun in bf16, Gemma-3-27B identifies the sequence for \vfourEoneOwnAIAGemmaTwentySevenBf{}\% with the image and \vfourEoneTextAIAGemmaTwentySevenBf{}\% without (against \vfourEoneOwnAIAGemmaTwentySeven{}\% at 4-bit; Table~\ref{tab:ctrl}): the interval includes zero, so the rerun did not restore AIA performance, and its LIL and DSCR intervals also include zero. Because precision and serving stack changed together, we draw no conclusion about quantization. A different-family bf16/vLLM model, Pixtral-12B, is likewise near chance on sequence (\vfourEoneOwnAIAPixtral{}\% against \vfourEoneTextAIAPixtral{}\%) and lesion side, with no detected donor tracking (DSCR image arms excluded; Appendix~\ref{sec:app-handling}), so 4-bit Ollama serving is an unlikely sole cause, though scale and training still differ (full intervals and donor gains in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-tables}).

Reading each item against itself (Tables~\ref{tab:e3} and~\ref{tab:e3trans}), replacing the stated category moves the answer for a minority of informative items in three models and a slim majority in Gemma-3-27B (\vfourTransLabelMovedGemmaTwentySeven{} of \vfourTransLabelInfGemmaTwentySeven{}); crossing the 12-week window moves it most for Llama-4-Scout and not at all for Gemma-3-27B, and MedGemma-4B answers the most common class almost regardless. Stating the rule in the prompt (post hoc) raises accuracy to \vfourExplAccGemmaTwelve{}\% for Gemma-3-12B and \vfourExplAccGemmaTwentySeven{}\% for Gemma-3-27B (from \vfourExplGoldGemmaTwelve{}\% unstated; paired gains \vfourExplDiffGemmaTwelve{} [\vfourExplLoGemmaTwelve{}, \vfourExplHiGemmaTwelve{}] and \vfourExplDiffGemmaTwentySeven{} [\vfourExplLoGemmaTwentySeven{}, \vfourExplHiGemmaTwentySeven{}]) and to \vfourExplAccScout{}\% for Llama-4-Scout (\vfourExplDiffScout{}), while MedGemma-4B does not move (\vfourExplDiffMedGemma{}); all stay at or below the best category lookup, so a supplied rule is followed incompletely even when stated (Appendix~\ref{sec:app-handling}).

\paragraph{Chain (secondary).} The ordinary chain matters only for TCM, through the stated facts above; other image-minus-text differences are small with intervals including zero, so we claim no causal-degradation effect across phases.











\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Accuracy on an expert-labeled brain-MRI chain cannot show whether a model reads the scan when the labels are not validated targets for what is displayed: an automated rule reproduces the expert RANO rating for \vfourConcordantDetPct{}\% of determinate follow-ups, and the source benchmark did not establish same-patient chains. On a LUMIERE chain with a stated key origin and fixed controls, a reference model reads sequence and lesion side, whereas four open-weight models show limited, inconsistent image dependence: one accuracy gain survives correction but stays below the most common key's share, donor-specific tracking is not consistently detected, and none exceeds the best category lookup. These results do not identify internal image use; clinician review and a second reference model remain outstanding.



\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Clinician review is outstanding.} No clinician has verified that each question is answerable from the displayed inputs or that each key is correct. LIL and DSCR are derived from DeepBraTumIA segmentation masks unseen by the model, so segmentation errors propagate directly to these targets; the reference model supports task feasibility, not item-level validity. The enhancement rule further omits T2/FLAIR change, clinical status, and steroid dose, abstains when no measurable enhancing disease exists (\vfourUndetermined{} of \vfourFollowups{} follow-ups), and matches the expert category for \vfourConcordantDetPct{}\% of determinate cases. Disagreement may reflect segmentation or measurement error, a different target lesion or reference scan, simplification of the rule, or evidence outside the displayed slices, which this audit cannot distinguish.

Our complete-response label is also weaker than clinical RANO: it is assigned whenever follow-up enhancement falls below the measurability threshold, potentially including residual sub-threshold disease, rather than requiring sustained disappearance of all enhancing and nonmeasurable disease. The rule tracks only the enhancing component nearest the baseline target lesion and does not implement RANO's new-lesion criterion, under which an unequivocal new lesion implies progressive disease; an audit of the follow-ups the rule labels complete response but the expert labels progressive disease (Appendix~\ref{sec:app-summary}) finds that this missing criterion accounts for some but not all of the disagreement. The expert rating is recorded for audit purposes but is not used as the benchmark key.

\textbf{The management key is a rule, not a documented treatment.} LUMIERE records no treatment after chemoradiotherapy. The TCM key therefore assumes a radiotherapy schedule ending 10 weeks after surgery and simplifies the 12-week RANO window, which can still permit progression when new enhancement lies outside the radiation field. PJRF further assumes that survival time and scan weeks share the surgery-week origin, while censoring is undocumented. With only \vfourPjrfN{} patients, a leave-one-out logistic model using the stated facts did not beat the base rate (Brier \vfourPjrfBrierLR{} vs.\ \vfourPjrfBrierBase{}). This does not imply that the facts lack predictive information: the base rate reflects our stratified sample, and the outcome construction itself is unverified. We therefore treat PJRF as secondary and make no substantive claim from it.

\textbf{Power and scope.} With \vfourNLIL{} to \vfourNAIA{} patients per phase, the pre-specified $\pm10$-point margin is attainable only when few items differ between arms, so several comparisons remain inconclusive. Only one E1 image-effect test (Llama-4-Scout on DSCR) survives Holm correction. Moreover, MedGemma-4B, Gemma-3-12B, and Gemma-3-27B fail the preregistered AIA positive-control gate; under the analysis plan, their LIL and DSCR effects therefore support no conclusion. AIA tests only sequence identification, so passing it does not establish lesion reading. Donor images are from different patients and thus differ in anatomy beyond the manipulated feature, while text-only stems still refer to images.

The study uses a single-center, deliberately stratified cohort. The four open-weight models were served at 4-bit through Ollama, whereas the reference model is an API system of unknown size and precision, so between-model differences confound scale, training, quantization, and serving stack and cannot be attributed specifically to shortcut learning. bf16 reruns of Gemma-3-27B and a different-family model, Pixtral-12B, through vLLM also remained near chance on AIA, making 4-bit Ollama serving an unlikely sole explanation but not isolating quantization from serving, scale, or training; only these two models were rerun. The reference model was evaluated at temperature 0, once per condition on the original donor assignment (the swap condition was additionally rerun under alternative assignments; Appendix~\ref{sec:app-summary}), and API models may change over time.

\textbf{Input handling was checked only in part.} Post hoc checks confirmed that every image increased prompt token count, including the second and third DSCR images, and that each pipeline answered known-answer probes correctly (Appendix~\ref{sec:app-handling}). We did not verify that each serving stack passes the same image representation to its encoder as a reference implementation, and a single-patient check cannot establish preservation of MRI contrast across encoders. Thus, near-chance sequence identification in several open models remains unexplained: dropped images appear unlikely, but weak visual features or default-answer behavior remain possible. MedGemma-4B produced invalid JSON for \vfourParseInvalidMedGemma{} of \vfourParseNMedGemma{} records and Llama-4-Scout for \vfourParseInvalidScout{} of \vfourParseNScout{}; pattern matching recovered the answer letter except for \vfourParseNoLetterMedGemma{} MedGemma records (Appendix~\ref{sec:app-handling}). Intervals treat patients as exchangeable.

\paragraph{Audit size check.} Where the rater recorded target-lesion diameters (\vfourSizeN{} follow-ups, \vfourSizeExpertPD{} of them rated progressive disease), agreement was \vfourSizeCloseAgree{} of \vfourSizeCloseN{} where our product was within a factor of 1.5 of the rater's and \vfourSizeOtherAgree{} of \vfourSizeOtherN{} otherwise, a gap this subset is too small to establish as a real difference, so it neither rules in nor rules out size discrepancy as an explanation of the disagreements; it also has few stable-disease or partial-response ratings, where disagreement concentrates, so it cannot test them.

\paragraph{New-lesion disagreements (audit).} The enhancement rule does not implement RANO's new-lesion criterion, under which an unequivocal new lesion implies progressive disease. Among the \vfourNewLesionN{} follow-ups the rule labels complete response but the expert labels progressive disease, \vfourNewLesionPlausible{} contain a distinct newly measurable enhancing component elsewhere, \vfourNewLesionPreexisting{} contain a similarly sized component already present at the nadir, and \vfourNewLesionNone{} contain no other measurable enhancing component, so the missing criterion accounts for some but not all of the disagreement.

\paragraph{Donor dependence (post hoc).} AIA, LIL and DSCR use \vfourDonorNAIAMedGemma{}, \vfourDonorNLILMedGemma{} and \vfourDonorNDSCRMedGemma{} distinct donors respectively, none used more than \vfourDonorMaxAIAMedGemma{} times; because donors are reused across recipients, recipients that share a donor are not independent, so a naive component bootstrap is uninformative. We instead resample recipients that share a donor together, drop each donor in turn, and permute donor keys among recipients (never onto a recipient's own key; $p$ is the share with a gain at least as large). The permutation is a conditional label-association test and does not reconstruct the image-assignment mechanism. Draws whose repair of an own-key collision fails are discarded, so the accepted count is at most the 5,000 attempted: \vfourDonorPermNAIAMedGemma{} for the AIA cell below and \vfourDonorPermNDSCRScout{} for the DSCR cell (AIA and LIL reach 5,000; DSCR discards some). MedGemma-4B on AIA: gain \vfourEtwoGainAIAMedGemma{}, donor-cluster interval [\vfourDonorLoAIAMedGemma{}, \vfourDonorHiAIAMedGemma{}], leave-one-donor-out \vfourDonorLooLoAIAMedGemma{} to \vfourDonorLooHiAIAMedGemma{}, permutation $p=\vfourDonorPermAIAMedGemma{}$. Llama-4-Scout on DSCR: gain \vfourEtwoGainDSCRScout{}, interval [\vfourDonorLoDSCRScout{}, \vfourDonorHiDSCRScout{}], leave-one-donor-out \vfourDonorLooLoDSCRScout{} to \vfourDonorLooHiDSCRScout{}, permutation $p=\vfourDonorPermDSCRScout{}$. No permutation $p$ of the twelve open-model cells is below .05.

\paragraph{Protocol validation with known-behaviour predictors.} To illustrate that the donor-key permutation discriminates correct donor-specific tracking from a generic response to any image, we ran the same gain and permutation on synthetic predictors with known behaviour, on the real DSCR items and donor assignment (\vfourDonorValN{} recipients; \vfourDonorValSims{} simulations; detection rates reported with Wilson intervals against a nominal 5\% level; no model is called). The permutation is the conditional label-association test of the donor-dependence check above, with the gain computed on the option texts so it does not depend on the lettering (options are already shuffled per item at load); it does not preserve the equal-image-count strata or keep a reused donor's keys together, so it licenses only that narrow comparison. Because draws that cannot repair an own-key collision are discarded, the permutation samples a conditional null whose sampling distribution we do not derive analytically; we instead show by simulation that its false-positive rate stays at the nominal level and that it matches an independent repair-free sampler (the repaired-permutation validity check in Section~\ref{sec:app-summary}). A constant answer gives no gain and a uniformly random answer a negligible one (\vfourDonorValRandGain{} points), and neither is flagged in any run (\vfourDonorValConstDetect{}\%; Table~\ref{tab:donorval}). Three no-tracking predictors that respond to image presence but not to which donor is shown serve as false-positive checks: a generic image shift (the most common donor class whenever an image is present) reaches a positive gain of \vfourDonorValShiftGain{} points yet is never flagged (\vfourDonorValShiftDetect{}\%), and predictors keyed to the option position or to the number of scans shown are flagged in no more than \vfourDonorValPositionDetectHi{}\% of runs (upper Wilson bound), consistent with the nominal 5\% level, though the simulation count leaves the interval too wide to establish a rate below it. A genuine tracker that returns the shown donor's key with probability $\rho$ is flagged in \vfourDonorValTrackFullDetect{}\% of runs at $\rho=1$ and \vfourDonorValTrackLowDetect{}\% at $\rho=0.4$ (its effective probability of emitting the donor key is $\rho+(1-\rho)/4=0.55$, since the guess branch can also land on it). The same separation holds on AIA and LIL (the generic shift never flagged; the noisy tracker flagged in \vfourDonorValTrackLowDetectAIA{}\% and \vfourDonorValTrackLowDetectLIL{}\% of runs) and across re-drawn valid assignments (Table~\ref{tab:donorval} gives DSCR). The gain alone does not separate tracking from a generic image response, since the generic shift also has a positive gain; the permutation does, flagging the tracker but not the shift. These checks illustrate the test's discrimination; a non-significant result for the open models indicates that we did not detect donor-specific tracking and does not rule out weaker or differently structured tracking.

\paragraph{Validity of the collision-repaired permutation (post hoc).} A uniformly drawn whole permutation almost never avoids every own-key collision (\vfourPermValWholeAccDSCRScout{} of \vfourPermValWholeDraws{} draws are collision-free for the DSCR cell), so rejecting colliding draws outright is infeasible and the test repairs them instead; we check that the repair does not distort the null (no model is called). The null hypothesis is that a recipient's answer is independent of which donor's key it was assigned; we hold the observed swap and text-only answers fixed and treat the donor-key labels as exchangeable among recipients, subject to the constraint that no recipient receives its own key (the swap construction forbids it). This is a conditional label-association test, not a test of the image-assignment mechanism. Drawing a fresh constraint-valid donor assignment for the real answers as a no-tracking null, the repaired test shows no excess false positives in these simulations (rejection at or below the nominal 5\% over \vfourPermValSims{} null datasets): \vfourPermValTypeIDSCRScout{}\% [\vfourPermValTypeILoDSCRScout{}, \vfourPermValTypeIHiDSCRScout{}] on DSCR (Llama-4-Scout) and \vfourPermValTypeIAIAMedGemma{}\% [\vfourPermValTypeILoAIAMedGemma{}, \vfourPermValTypeIHiAIAMedGemma{}] on AIA (MedGemma-4B), with median null $p$ \vfourPermValMedPDSCRScout{} and \vfourPermValMedPAIAMedGemma{}. On the real answers the repair sampler and an independently constructed repair-free sampler (sequential constrained assignment, which never collides) induce similar empirical null gain distributions (means \vfourPermValNullMeanDSCRScout{} vs \vfourPermValNullMeanSeqDSCRScout{} points on DSCR and \vfourPermValNullMeanAIAMedGemma{} vs \vfourPermValNullMeanSeqAIAMedGemma{} on AIA; the two distribution functions differ by at most \vfourPermValKSGapDSCRScout{} and \vfourPermValKSGapAIAMedGemma{} points) and give close permutation $p$ (\vfourPermValRepairDSCRScout{} vs \vfourPermValSeqDSCRScout{}; \vfourPermValRepairAIAMedGemma{} vs \vfourPermValSeqAIAMedGemma{}). In these simulations the repaired test holds its false-positive rate at the nominal level and returns the same result as the repair-free sampler; we do not claim validity beyond the no-tracking mechanisms simulated here.

\paragraph{Robustness to the donor assignment (post hoc).} Many assignments satisfy the swap constraints, so we re-ran the models under \vfourRobustGeminiNAssign{} further distinct valid assignments, drawn by the same procedure, and recomputed the gain and permutation. The reference model stays strongly significant on both phases (permutation $p<.001$ in \vfourRobustGeminiAIANSig{} of \vfourRobustGeminiNAssign{} AIA and \vfourRobustGeminiLILNSig{} of \vfourRobustGeminiNAssign{} LIL assignments; gain \vfourRobustGeminiAIAGainMin{} to \vfourRobustGeminiAIAGainMax{} on AIA and \vfourRobustGeminiLILGainMin{} to \vfourRobustGeminiLILGainMax{} on LIL). Llama-4-Scout on DSCR, the one open-model cell with a positive gain, is significant in only \vfourRobustScoutDSCRNSig{} of \vfourRobustScoutNAssign{} assignments (gain \vfourRobustScoutDSCRGainMin{} to \vfourRobustScoutDSCRGainMax{}, permutation $p$ from \vfourRobustScoutDSCRPermPMin{} to \vfourRobustScoutDSCRPermPMax{}), so donor-specific tracking is not consistently detected for this cell and the one significant assignment is sensitive to the particular donor draw.
